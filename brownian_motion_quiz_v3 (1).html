Moments & Itô isometry

Q1–3 · 6 · 15 · 88

\(B_t\sim N(0,t)\); scaling \(B_s\overset{d}{=}\sqrt{s}\,B_1\).

Even moments only: \(E[B_t^{2n}]=(2n-1)!!\,t^{\,n}\), odd moments \(=0\).

\(E[B_t^2]=t,\quad E[B_t^4]=3t^2,\quad E[B_t^6]=15t^3\)

Itô isometry: \(\;E\!\big[(\int_0^t H_s\,dB_s)^2\big]=\int_0^t E[H_s^2]\,ds.\)

Polarisation: \(\;E\!\big[\int H\,dB\int K\,dB\big]=\int_0^t E[H_sK_s]\,ds.\)

Every \(\int H\,dB\) has mean \(0\), so isometry hands you the variance directly. For deterministic \(H=f(s)\): just integrate \(f(s)^2\). Double factorial: \(3!!=3,\ 5!!=15\).

Covariance · conditioning · bridges

Q7–8 · 20 · 24 · 38 · 49 · 59

\(\operatorname{cov}(B_s,B_t)=s\wedge t\). Increment covariance \(=\) overlap length: \(\operatorname{cov}(B_b-B_a,B_d-B_c)=|(a,b]\cap(c,d]|\).

Split \(B_t=B_s+\Delta\), \(\Delta\perp\mathcal F_s\), \(\Delta\sim N(0,t-s)\); then \(E[B_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=B_s\).

Backward projection: \(E[B_s\mid B_t]=\tfrac st B_t\), \(\operatorname{var}(B_s\mid B_t)=\tfrac{s(t-s)}{t}\).

Bridge \(X_t=B_t-tB_1\): \(\operatorname{cov}(X_s,X_t)=s(1-t)\), \(\operatorname{var}=t(1-t)\).

Add & subtract: write the later value as earlier \(+\) independent increment, expand, and kill every term with an odd leftover moment.

Martingale dictionary

Q4–5 · 25 · 28 · 56 · 82 · 95

Mean check: a martingale has constant mean. If \(E[X_t]\) grows \(\Rightarrow\) sub-martingale.

  • \(B_t,\quad B_t^2-t,\quad B_t^3-3tB_t\)
  • \(e^{\theta B_t-\theta^2 t/2},\quad e^{t/2}\cos B_t,\quad \int_0^t H\,dB\)

Match the drift: for \(X_t=B_t+\mu t\), \(e^{\theta X_t}\) is a martingale iff \(\theta=-2\mu\).

Gaussian transfer: swap \(t\!\to\!\Gamma(t,t)=E[M_t^2]\); \(\langle M\rangle_t=\Gamma(t,t)\). Doob \(L^2\): \(E[\sup_{s\le T}M_s^2]\le 4E[M_T^2]\).

To build a martingale, cancel the drift. Convex function of a martingale (e.g. \(e^{B_t},B_t^2,|B_t|\)) is a sub-martingale (Jensen).

Itô's lemma — master template

Q17–18 · 29 · 64–66 · 80–87 · 100–105
\(dt\,dt=0,\quad dt\,dB=0,\quad dB\,dB=dt\)

\(df(B_t)=f'(B_t)\,dB_t+\tfrac12 f''(B_t)\,dt.\)

For \(dX=b\,dt+\sigma\,dB\): drift of \(df(t,X)\) is \(\partial_t f+b\,\partial_x f+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial_{xx}f\).

Martingale ⇔ heat eq.: \(f(B_t,t)\) is a martingale iff \(\partial_t f+\tfrac12\partial_{xx}f=0\).

Feynman–Kac: \(u=E[g(B_t)\mid B_0=x]\) solves \(\partial_t u=\tfrac12\partial_{xx}u\). Core identity \(\int_0^tB\,dB=\tfrac{B_t^2-t}{2}\).

All of Itô is the \(\tfrac12 f''\,dt\) term. If a factor is smooth there is no correction: \(d(tB_t)=B_t\,dt+t\,dB_t\).

SDE toolkit

Q61–63 · 67–68 · 90–94 · 97–98 · 103–104

GBM \(dS=\mu S\,dt+\sigma S\,dB\):

\(S_t=S_0e^{(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2)t+\sigma B_t}\)

\(E[S_t]=S_0e^{\mu t}\); median \(=S_0e^{(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2)t}\); \(\operatorname{var}=S_0^2e^{2\mu t}(e^{\sigma^2t}-1)\); martingale iff \(\mu=0\).

OU \(dX=-\theta X\,dt+\sigma\,dB\): \(X_t=X_0e^{-\theta t}+\sigma\!\int_0^t e^{-\theta(t-s)}dB_s\), \(\operatorname{var}\to\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}\), \(\operatorname{cov}(X_s,X_t)=\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}e^{-\theta|t-s|}\).

Vasicek \(dr=a(b-r)dt+\sigma dW\): OU reverting to \(b\). Bridge: \(db_t=-\tfrac{b_t}{1-t}dt+dB_t\).

OU / Vasicek → multiply by \(e^{\theta t}\) (deterministic ⇒ no Itô term). GBM → take \(\log\). Then isometry gives the variance.

Stopping · reflection · exit

Q26–27 · 48 · 53–54 · 56 · 78–79 · 99

Reflection: \(P(\max_{s\le t}B_s\ge a)=2P(B_t\ge a)=2\big(1-\Phi(\tfrac{a}{\sqrt t})\big)\).

Running max: \(M_t\overset{d}{=}|B_t|\), \(E[M_t]=\sqrt{2t/\pi}\).

Hitting time \(T_a\): \(T_a<\infty\) a.s. but \(E[T_a]=\infty\); \(E[e^{-\lambda T_a}]=e^{-a\sqrt{2\lambda}}\).

Exit of \((-a,b)\): \(P(\text{hit }b\text{ first})=\tfrac{a}{a+b}\), \(E[T]=ab\).

Exit interval → probabilities are the cross-ratio \(a/(a+b)\); \(E[T]=ab\) drops out of \(E[B_T^2]=E[T]\). Optional stopping needs finite \(E[T]\) — one-sided \(T_a\) is the classic failure.

The "why" behind the traps. When a computation feels like magic (or seems to prove something false), one of these pictures is usually the resolution.

Why the Itô correction exists

Over a step \(dt\), Brownian motion moves by \(\sim\sqrt{dt}\), so \((\Delta B)^2\sim dt\) is first order, not negligible. Ordinary Taylor drops \((\Delta X)^2\); here it survives as \(\tfrac12 f''(dB)^2=\tfrac12 f''\,dt\). That single surviving term is Itô's lemma — everything else is classical calculus. The operative rule is \((dB)^2=dt\).

Mean vs. almost-sure limit

For \(e^{B_t-ct}\): since \(B_t/t\to0\) a.s., the exponent \(\to-\infty\) and the typical path dies to \(0\) for any \(c>0\). Yet \(E[e^{B_t-ct}]=e^{(\frac12-c)t}\) can explode when \(c<\tfrac12\): a handful of astronomically large paths drag the average up. Always read whether the question asks for the a.s. limit or the mean.

Conditioning earlier on later shrinks by s/t

\(E[B_s\mid B_t]=\tfrac st B_t\), not \(B_t\). Knowing only the endpoint \(B_t\), your best guess for the midpoint is a fraction \(s/t\) of the way there — a straight line back to the origin. The leftover wiggle has variance \(s(t-s)/t\), the Brownian-bridge variance, vanishing at both ends.

Quadratic variation ignores drift

A drift term is smooth: over \(dt\) it moves \(O(dt)\), so its square is \(O(dt^2)\) — negligible. Only the jittery martingale part accumulates, at rate \((dB)^2=dt\). Hence \(\langle B+\mu t\rangle_t=\langle B\rangle_t=t\), and for \(dX=b\,dt+\sigma\,dB\) only \(\sigma^2\) matters: \(\langle X\rangle_t=\int_0^t\sigma_s^2\,ds\).

Running max has the same law as |Bₜ|

Reflection principle: any path that touches \(a\) and ends below has a mirror-image twin (reflected after the first hit) that ends above. Pairing them gives \(P(\max\ge a)=2P(B_t\ge a)\), and letting the level vary shows \(\max_{s\le t}B_s\overset{d}{=}|B_t|\). Interactive sandbox #1 draws exactly this mirror.

The "optional stopping proves 0 = 1" trap. For \(T=\inf\{t:B_t=1\}\), \(E[B_T]=1\neq 0=E[B_0]\). No paradox: optional stopping needs \(E[T]<\infty\) (or uniform integrability), and here \(E[T]=\infty\). If a slick argument "shows" \(1=0\), an unbounded stopping time is the culprit.
Mean > median for GBM. \(\log S_t\) is symmetric (Gaussian), so the median sits at its centre \((\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2)t\). Exponentiating stretches the upper tail, lifting the mean above the median by the factor \(e^{\sigma^2 t/2}\). Don't report \(e^{\mu t}\) when asked for the median.
