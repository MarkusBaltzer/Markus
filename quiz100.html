01 Transformations
Which of the following processes is a standard Brownian motion?
By the scaling property \(cB_{t/c^2}\) is a standard BM. For \(\sqrt2\,B_{t/2}\) take \(c=\sqrt2\): \[\operatorname{var}(\sqrt2\,B_{t/2})=2\cdot\tfrac{t}{2}=t.\] The others have variance \(4t,\;2t,\;t/4\) or non-stationary increments.
02 Transformations
For which constant \(a>0\) is \((5B_{t/a})_{t\ge0}\) a standard Brownian motion?
Require \(\operatorname{var}(5B_{t/a})=25\cdot\tfrac{t}{a}=t\), i.e. \(a=25\). This is the scaling identity \(cB_{t/c^2}\) with \(c=5\).
03 Transformations
The process \(M_t=3B_t\) is:
\(M_t=\sigma B_t\) (here \(\sigma=3\)) is Gaussian, centred and a martingale, but \(\operatorname{var}(M_t)=9t\ne t\), so it is not a standard BM. Same distinction as the scaled martingale \(M_t=\sigma B_t\) in 4.1(a).
04 Transformations
Let \(X_t=2B_t\). What is the distribution of \(X_3-X_1\)?
\(X_3-X_1=2(B_3-B_1)\), and \(B_3-B_1\sim N(0,2)\). Scaling by \(2\) multiplies the variance by \(4\): \[\operatorname{var}(X_3-X_1)=4\cdot2=8.\]
05 Transformations
Let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) and \(X_t=\sqrt{t}\,Z\). Which statement is correct?
The one-dimensional marginals match (\(X_t\sim N(0,t)\)), but increments \(X_{t}-X_{s}=(\sqrt t-\sqrt s)Z\) and \(X_s=\sqrt s\,Z\) are multiples of the same \(Z\): perfectly correlated, hence not independent. So \(X\) is not a BM (cf. 2.5).
06 Transformations
Fix \(s>0\). The process \((B_{s+t}-B_s)_{t\ge0}\) is:
By stationarity and independence of increments, \((B_{s+t}-B_s)_{t\ge0}\) is again a standard BM, and it is independent of \(\mathcal F_s^B\) (2.3(c)). This is the Markov/restart property.
07 Transformations
Which of the following is not a Brownian motion?
Reflection \(-B_t\), scaling \(\sqrt2 B_{t/2}\) and \(3B_{t/9}\) (variance \(9\cdot t/9=t\)) and the restarted process are all standard BMs. But \(|B_t|\ge0\) is not Gaussian and cannot be a BM — reflection means negating the path, not taking absolute value.
08 Inversion & limits
Let \(W_t=tB_{1/t}\) for \(t>0\) and \(W_0=0\). Compute \(\operatorname{cov}(W_2,W_5)\).
\(W\) is the time-inverted Brownian motion, itself a standard BM: \[\operatorname{cov}(W_s,W_t)=st\,E[B_{1/s}B_{1/t}]=st\min(\tfrac1s,\tfrac1t)=s\wedge t.\] Hence \(\operatorname{cov}(W_2,W_5)=2\) (2.4(b)).
09 Inversion & limits
What is \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{B_t}{t}\) almost surely?
By the strong law of large numbers applied to the i.i.d. unit increments, \(B_t/t\to0\) a.s. (2.4(a)).
10 Inversion & limits
Why is \(W_t=tB_{1/t}\) continuous at \(t=0\) (with \(W_0=0\))?
Continuity for \(t>0\) is clear. At \(0\) substitute \(u=1/t\to\infty\): \[\lim_{t\downarrow0}tB_{1/t}=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{B_u}{u}=0=W_0\ \text{a.s.}\] using the SLLN limit from 2.4(a) (see 2.4(b)).
11 Inversion & limits
For \(X_t=B_t+\mu t\) with \(\mu<0\), what is \(\lim_{t\to\infty}X_t\) almost surely?
Write \(X_t=t\big(\tfrac{B_t}{t}+\mu\big)\). Since \(B_t/t\to0\) a.s., the bracket \(\to\mu<0\), so \(X_t\to-\infty\) a.s. (2.4(d)).
12 Inversion & limits
Compute \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{B_t+5t}{t}\) almost surely.
\(\dfrac{B_t+5t}{t}=\dfrac{B_t}{t}+5\to0+5=5\) a.s., since \(B_t/t\to0\) (2.4(a)).
13 Brownian bridge
For the Brownian bridge \(b_t=B_t-tB_1\) on \([0,1]\), what is \(E[b_t]\)?
\(E[b_t]=E[B_t]-tE[B_1]=0-0=0\) (2.6(b)). The bridge is a centred Gaussian process.

\(t(1-t)\) is a downward parabola maximised at \(t=\tfrac12\), where the variance is \(\tfrac14\).
18 Brownian bridge
Which process has the same finite-dimensional distributions as the Brownian bridge \(b_t=B_t-tB_1\)?
\(\tilde b_t=(1-t)B_{t/(1-t)}\) is centred Gaussian with \[\operatorname{cov}(\tilde b_s,\tilde b_t)=(1-s)(1-t)\min\!\Big(\tfrac{s}{1-s},\tfrac{t}{1-t}\Big)=s(1-t),\] matching the bridge's covariance, hence the same law (2.6(d)).
19 Correlated BMs
Let \(B=(B^{(1)},B^{(2)})\) be independent BMs. For which \(\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\) is \(\lambda_1B^{(1)}+\lambda_2B^{(2)}\) a standard Brownian motion?
\(\langle\lambda,B\rangle\) has increment variance \(\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle(t-s)\), so it is standard iff \(\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle=1\). Only \((3/5,4/5)\) satisfies \((3/5)^2+(4/5)^2=1\) (3.2(a)).
20 Correlated BMs
With \(W^{(1)}=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,B^{(1)}+\rho B^{(2)}\) and \(\rho=0.6\), what is \(\operatorname{corr}(W^{(1)}_t,B^{(2)}_t)\)?
\(\operatorname{cov}(W^{(1)}_t,B^{(2)}_t)=\rho t\) and both variances equal \(t\), so the correlation is exactly \(\rho=0.6\) (3.3(b)).
21 Correlated BMs

22 Correlated BMs
With \(W^{(1)}=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,B^{(1)}+\rho B^{(2)}\), which process is a martingale?
Expanding, \(W^{(1)}_tB^{(2)}_t=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,B^{(1)}_tB^{(2)}_t+\rho\big((B^{(2)}_t)^2-t\big)+\rho t\). Both bracketed terms are martingales, so subtracting the drift \(\rho t\) gives the martingale \(W^{(1)}_tB^{(2)}_t-\rho t\) (3.3(c)).
23 Correlated BMs
Let \(B^{(1)},B^{(2)},B^{(3)}\) be independent BMs. Which process is a martingale?
\(\|B_t\|^2-dt=\sum_{i=1}^d\big((B^{(i)}_t)^2-t\big)\) is a sum of martingales, hence a martingale. For \(d=3\) the compensator is \(3t\) (3.2(b)).
24 Correlated BMs
