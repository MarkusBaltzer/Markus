Envelope Factor Workbook

What the \(\|F_n\|\) in the bracketing condition is doing, and a fixed routine for solving it at the exam: keep the fineness \(\delta\) as an unknown, compute two numbers, divide, and solve for \(\delta\) on the last line.

1What the factor actually is

In Theorem 1.20 (bracketing CLT for changing classes) the brackets are not required to have width \(\le\varepsilon\). They are required to have width \(\le\varepsilon\,\|F_n\|_{L^2}\):

\[ \mathbb E\Big[\sup_{s,t\in\text{cell}_j}|f_{n,t}-f_{n,s}|^2\Big]\;\le\;\varepsilon^2\,\|F_n\|_{L^2}^2 . \]

That is a relative width. Divide both sides by \(\|F_n\|^2\) and the condition becomes

\[ \boxed{\;\frac{\|u_j-l_j\|_{L^2}^2}{\|F_n\|_{L^2}^2}\;\le\;\varepsilon^2\;} \]

So the whole "insert the factor" business is just this: compute the ratio of two numbers and make it smaller than \(\varepsilon^2\). Nothing else. The book writes it as a chain of equalities where \(\|F_n\|\) is "inserted" at some point, but that chain is only a way of presenting this ratio.

Why relative?

The functions in these classes carry normalisers that change with \(n\): \(h_n^{-1/2}\), \(\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}\), \((h_n\bar F^x(u_n^x))^{-1/2}\). Measuring widths relative to the envelope makes the statement independent of those normalisers. In all the book's examples \(\|F_n\|^2\) converges to a positive constant \(c\), so "relative" just means "divide by \(c\)".

2The routine: four quantities, then solve

Keep the fineness as an unknown δ from the start. There are two natural kinds of unknown:

  • Mass version: "choose \(t_0\lt\dots\lt t_m\) so that each cell has limit mass \(\le\delta\)". Works on any index set, including \([0,\infty)\).
  • Mesh version: "equidistant grid with \(t_j-t_{j-1}=\delta\)". Only for a compact index set \([a,b]\) where the limit function has a bounded derivative. Easier to picture, slightly worse constants.
AWidth²

Compute \(\|u_j-l_j\|^2\) and its limit. Bound it by something in δ: \(A_n\le A(\delta)+o(1)\).

BEnvelope²

Compute \(\|F_n\|^2\to c\gt0\). No δ here.

RRatio

\(R_n=A_n/B_n\to A(\delta)/c\). The normalising constants usually cancel.

SSolve

Set \(A(\delta)/c=\varepsilon^2/2\) and solve for δ. Then \(R_n\le\varepsilon^2\) for \(n\ge n_0\).

Then one more line: count. With \(\delta\) now a known function of \(\varepsilon\), the number of cells is \(m\le(\text{total mass})/\delta+1\) (mass version) or \(m=\lceil(b-a)/\delta\rceil\) (mesh version). You need \(m\le C\varepsilon^{-p}\) for some \(p\), and then the entropy integral is finite.

Why solve for \(\varepsilon^2/2\) and not \(\varepsilon^2\)

\(R_n\) only converges to \(A(\delta)/c\). If you set the limit equal to \(\varepsilon^2\) exactly, \(R_n\) may sit slightly above \(\varepsilon^2\) for every \(n\). Setting the limit to \(\varepsilon^2/2\) leaves a gap, and since \(R_n\to\) limit, \(R_n\le\varepsilon^2\) for \(n\) large. Any number below 1 works instead of \(\tfrac12\).

Why the \(o(1)\) is uniform over cells

For fixed \(\varepsilon\), \(\delta\) is fixed, so the grid is a fixed finite set of points not depending on \(n\). Convergence at each of those finitely many points is automatically uniform over them. That is also why (1.8) can be skipped.

3Reading the book's one-liner

Here is Theorem 3.10 (tail empirical process). Left: the book's chain. Right: the same thing done with the ratio. They are the same computation.

Book
\[\begin{aligned} \|u_j-l_j\|&=\big(T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)\big)^{1/2}(1+o(1))\\ &=\big(T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)\big)^{1/2}\,a^{\frac1{2\gamma}}\,\|F_n\|\,(1+o(1))\\ &\le\varepsilon\|F_n\|. \end{aligned}\]

Line 2 "inserts the factor": multiply by \(1=a^{\frac1{2\gamma}}\cdot a^{-\frac1{2\gamma}}\) and replace \(a^{-\frac1{2\gamma}}\) by \(\|F_n\|(1+o(1))\), because \(\|F_n\|^2\to a^{-1/\gamma}\).

Ratio method

Notation: \(T_n(s)=\bar F(u_ns)/\bar F(u_n)\), \(T(s)=s^{-1/\gamma}\), envelope \(F_n=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{Z\gt u_na\}\) so \(\|F_n\|^2=T_n(a)\).

\[\begin{aligned} A_n&=T_n(t_{j-1})-T_n(t_j)\to T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)\le\delta\\ B_n&=T_n(a)\to a^{-1/\gamma}\\ R_n&\to \delta\,a^{1/\gamma}\overset{!}{=}\varepsilon^2/2\\ \Rightarrow\ \delta&=\tfrac{\varepsilon^2}{2}a^{-1/\gamma}. \end{aligned}\]

No "inserting". The factor \(a^{-1/\gamma}\) appears by itself when you solve.

Takeaway

"Inserting \(\|F_n\|\)" = writing \(A_n = R_n\cdot B_n\). If you compute \(R_n\) directly, you never have to guess which factor to insert. At the exam, do the ratio on scrap paper, then write it in whichever form you like.

4Solver: tail process on \([a,b]\)

Class \(f_{n,t}(z)=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{z\gt u_nt\}\), \(t\in[a,b]\), with \(T_n(s)=\bar F(u_ns)/\bar F(u_n)\to T(s)=s^{-1/\gamma}\) and \(\|F_n\|^2=T_n(a)\to a^{-1/\gamma}\) (full derivation in Example 1). Pick the numbers and see each line of the routine filled in, for both kinds of unknown. The conditional tail (Theorem 3.23) gives the same answers with \(\gamma(x)\) in place of \(\gamma\), because \(g(x)\!\int\!K^2\) cancels in the ratio.

5Worked examples with δ unknown until the end

Every example is self-contained. It opens with a setting card that states the model, the notation (\(u_n\), \(T_n\), \(T\), \(R(K)\), …) and the facts you are allowed to quote. Then it walks through the routine one line at a time, with \(\|F_n\|^2\) and the width computed explicitly. The last line is the paragraph you would hand in.

Two facts used in every computation: an indicator squared is itself, \(1_A^2=1_A\), so \(\mathbb E\big[(c\,1_A)^2\big]=c^2P(A)\); and for sets \(A\subseteq B\), \(1_B-1_A=1_{B\setminus A}\).

Example 1 · Tail empirical process, mass version

Theorem 3.10
Setting & definitions
Data
\(Z_1,\dots,Z_n\) iid, positive, continuous cdf \(F\), survival function \(\bar F=1-F\).
Tail assumption
\(\bar F\) is regularly varying with index \(1/\gamma\gt0\): \(\bar F(z)=z^{-1/\gamma}\ell(z)\), \(\ell\) slowly varying. Equivalently \(\bar F(zs)/\bar F(z)\to s^{-1/\gamma}\) as \(z\to\infty\), for every \(s\gt0\).
Threshold
Intermediate sequence \(k=k_n\to\infty\), \(k/n\to0\), and \(u_n=F^{\leftarrow}(1-k/n)\to\infty\). By continuity \(n\bar F(u_n)=k\).
\(T_n\) and \(T\)
\[T_n(s)=\frac{\bar F(u_ns)}{\bar F(u_n)}=\frac{P(Z\gt u_ns)}{P(Z\gt u_n)},\qquad T(s)=s^{-1/\gamma}.\] \(T_n(s)\) is the conditional probability \(P(Z\gt u_ns\mid Z\gt u_n)\) and the mean of the tail empirical function \(\tilde T_n(s)=\frac1k\sum_i1\{Z_i\gt u_ns\}\). Regular variation (with \(u_n\to\infty\)) gives \(T_n(s)\to T(s)\) for each \(s\gt0\).
Index set
\(t\in[a,b]\) with \(0\lt a\lt b\lt\infty\).
  1. The class, and why it is this one
    \[f_{n,t}(z)=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}\,1\{z\gt u_nt\},\qquad \mathcal F_n=\{f_{n,t}:t\in[a,b]\}.\] Then \(\mathbb G^{(n)}f_{n,t}=\sqrt n\big(\tfrac1n\sum_if_{n,t}(Z_i)-\mathbb Ef_{n,t}\big)=\dfrac{\sum_i1\{Z_i\gt u_nt\}-n\bar F(u_nt)}{\sqrt{n\bar F(u_n)}}=\sqrt k\big(\tilde T_n(t)-T_n(t)\big)\), using \(n\bar F(u_n)=k\). So this empirical process is exactly the tail empirical process.
  2. Envelope
    For fixed \(z\), \(t\mapsto1\{z\gt u_nt\}\) is decreasing, so the sup over \(t\in[a,b]\) is at \(t=a\): \[F_n(z)=\sup_{t}|f_{n,t}(z)|=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{z\gt u_na\}.\]
  3. B: compute ‖F_n‖²
    \[\|F_n\|_{L^2}^2=\mathbb E\big[F_n(Z)^2\big]=\bar F(u_n)^{-1}\,\mathbb E\big[1\{Z\gt u_na\}^2\big]=\frac{P(Z\gt u_na)}{\bar F(u_n)}=\frac{\bar F(u_na)}{\bar F(u_n)}=T_n(a)\ \longrightarrow\ a^{-1/\gamma}.\]
    Squaring removes the \(-1/2\) power: \((\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2})^2=\bar F(u_n)^{-1}\). Squaring the indicator does nothing.
  4. Unknown δ and grid
    Let δ \(\gt0\), to be chosen. Take \(a=t_0\lt t_1\lt\dots\lt t_m=b\) with \(T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)\le\delta\) for all \(j\). Since \(T\) is continuous and decreasing, walking from \(a\) and stepping down \(\delta\) in \(T\)-value each time works, with \(m\le\dfrac{T(a)-T(b)}{\delta}+1\).
    The grid is built from the limit \(T\), not from \(T_n\). It depends on \(\delta\) (so on \(\varepsilon\)) only.
  5. Brackets
    Decreasing class, so for \(t\in[t_{j-1},t_j]\): \[l_j(z)=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{z\gt u_nt_j\}\ \le\ f_{n,t}(z)\ \le\ \bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{z\gt u_nt_{j-1}\}=u_j(z).\]
  6. A: compute the width²
    \(\{z\gt u_nt_j\}\subseteq\{z\gt u_nt_{j-1}\}\), so \(u_j-l_j=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{u_nt_{j-1}\lt z\le u_nt_j\}\) and \[\|u_j-l_j\|_{L^2}^2=\frac{P(u_nt_{j-1}\lt Z\le u_nt_j)}{\bar F(u_n)}=\frac{\bar F(u_nt_{j-1})-\bar F(u_nt_j)}{\bar F(u_n)}=T_n(t_{j-1})-T_n(t_j).\] At each of the finitely many grid points \(T_n\to T\), so \[\|u_j-l_j\|^2\ \longrightarrow\ T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)\ \le\ \delta.\]
  7. R: ratio
    \[\frac{\|u_j-l_j\|^2}{\|F_n\|^2}=\frac{T_n(t_{j-1})-T_n(t_j)}{T_n(a)}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)}{a^{-1/\gamma}}\ \le\ \delta\,a^{1/\gamma}.\]
  8. S: solve for δ
    Set the limit bound to \(\varepsilon^2/2\): \(\delta a^{1/\gamma}=\varepsilon^2/2\Rightarrow\) δ = ε² a^(−1/γ) / 2. Then for all \(j\) and \(n\ge n_0(\varepsilon)\): \(\|u_j-l_j\|^2\le\varepsilon^2\|F_n\|^2\).
  9. Count and entropy
    \(m\le\dfrac{T(a)-T(b)}{\delta}+1\le\dfrac{a^{-1/\gamma}}{\varepsilon^2a^{-1/\gamma}/2}+1=\dfrac2{\varepsilon^2}+1\). So \(N_{[\,]}(\varepsilon\|F_n\|,\mathcal F_n,L^2)\le2/\varepsilon^2+1\) and \(\int_0^{\delta_n}\sqrt{\log(2/\varepsilon^2+1)}\,d\varepsilon\to0\): (1.9). Grid free of \(n\): skip (1.8).
  10. Hand-in version

    Let \(T_n(s)=\bar F(u_ns)/\bar F(u_n)\) and \(T(s)=s^{-1/\gamma}\); by regular variation \(T_n(s)\to T(s)\) for every \(s\gt0\). The envelope of \(\mathcal F_n\) is \(F_n=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{Z\gt u_na\}\) with \(\|F_n\|^2=\bar F(u_na)/\bar F(u_n)=T_n(a)\to a^{-1/\gamma}\). Let \(\delta\gt0\) and choose \(a=t_0\lt\dots\lt t_m=b\) with \(T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)\le\delta\); this is possible with \(m\le a^{-1/\gamma}/\delta+1\). With \(l_j=f_{n,t_j}\le f_{n,t}\le f_{n,t_{j-1}}=u_j\) for \(t\in[t_{j-1},t_j]\),

    \[\frac{\|u_j-l_j\|^2}{\|F_n\|^2}=\frac{T_n(t_{j-1})-T_n(t_j)}{T_n(a)}\longrightarrow\frac{T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)}{a^{-1/\gamma}}\le\delta a^{1/\gamma},\]

    uniformly in \(j\) since there are finitely many grid points. With \(\delta=\varepsilon^2a^{-1/\gamma}/2\) the limit is at most \(\varepsilon^2/2\), so \(\|u_j-l_j\|\le\varepsilon\|F_n\|\) for all \(j\) and \(n\) large. Hence \(N_{[\,]}(\varepsilon\|F_n\|,\mathcal F_n,L^2)\le2/\varepsilon^2+1\), and (1.9) holds since \(\sqrt{\log(2/\varepsilon^2+1)}\) is integrable at 0. The partition does not depend on \(n\), so (1.8) is not needed.

Example 2 · Tail empirical process, mesh version

Theorem 3.10 with an equidistant grid
Setting & definitions
Data
\(Z_1,\dots,Z_n\) iid, positive, continuous cdf \(F\), \(\bar F=1-F\) regularly varying with index \(1/\gamma\gt0\).
Threshold
\(k\to\infty\), \(k/n\to0\), \(u_n=F^{\leftarrow}(1-k/n)\), so \(n\bar F(u_n)=k\).
\(T_n\) and \(T\)
\(T_n(s)=\bar F(u_ns)/\bar F(u_n)\to T(s)=s^{-1/\gamma}\) for each \(s\gt0\).
Derivative of \(T\)
\(T'(s)=-\tfrac1\gamma s^{-1/\gamma-1}\), so \(|T'|\) is decreasing and \(\sup_{s\ge a}|T'(s)|=\tfrac1\gamma a^{-1/\gamma-1}\).
Class
\(f_{n,t}(z)=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{z\gt u_nt\}\), \(t\in[a,b]\), \(0\lt a\lt b\lt\infty\).
  1. Envelope and ‖F_n‖²
    Decreasing in \(t\), sup at \(t=a\): \(F_n(z)=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{z\gt u_na\}\), and \[\|F_n\|^2=\frac{\mathbb E[1\{Z\gt u_na\}]}{\bar F(u_n)}=\frac{\bar F(u_na)}{\bar F(u_n)}=T_n(a)\to a^{-1/\gamma}.\]
  2. Unknown δ and grid
    Let δ \(\gt0\), \(t_j=a+j\delta\) for \(j=0,\dots,m-1\), \(t_m=b\), where \(m=\lceil(b-a)/\delta\rceil\).
  3. Brackets and width²
    Same as Example 1: \(l_j=f_{n,t_j}\), \(u_j=f_{n,t_{j-1}}\), and \[\|u_j-l_j\|^2=\frac{\bar F(u_nt_{j-1})-\bar F(u_nt_j)}{\bar F(u_n)}=T_n(t_{j-1})-T_n(t_j)\to T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j).\]
  4. A in terms of the mesh
    Mean value theorem / integral of the derivative: \[T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_j}\tfrac1\gamma s^{-1/\gamma-1}\,ds\le(t_j-t_{j-1})\sup_{s\ge a}\tfrac1\gamma s^{-1/\gamma-1}\le\tfrac1\gamma a^{-1/\gamma-1}\,\delta.\]
    This is the extra step of the mesh version: a length \(\delta\) is turned into a mass bound with the largest slope of \(T\) on \([a,b]\).
  5. R: ratio
    \[\frac{\|u_j-l_j\|^2}{\|F_n\|^2}\to\frac{T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)}{a^{-1/\gamma}}\le\frac{\tfrac1\gamma a^{-1/\gamma-1}\delta}{a^{-1/\gamma}}=\frac{\delta}{\gamma a}.\]
  6. S: solve for δ
    \(\dfrac{\delta}{\gamma a}=\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\Rightarrow\) δ = γ a ε² / 2.
  7. Count
    \(m=\Big\lceil\dfrac{b-a}{\delta}\Big\rceil=\Big\lceil\dfrac{2(b-a)}{\gamma a\,\varepsilon^2}\Big\rceil\le C\varepsilon^{-2}\) with \(C=2(b-a)/(\gamma a)+1\). Entropy integral as in Example 1.
Why the mesh version fails on \([a,\infty)\): \(m=\lceil(b-a)/\delta\rceil\) is infinite when \(b=\infty\). Use the mass version on unbounded index sets.

Example 3 · Kernel conditional cdf

Theorem 2.8 · mass version
Setting & definitions
Data
\((Y_i,X_i)\) iid in \([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R\). \(X\) has density \(f\), continuous at the fixed point \(x\), with \(f(x)\gt0\).
Conditional cdf
\(F(t\mid z)=P(Y\le t\mid X=z)\), with left limits \(F(t-\mid z)=P(Y\lt t\mid X=z)\). Assume continuity in the covariate uniformly in \(t\): \(\sup_t|F(t\mid z)-F(t\mid x)|\to0\) as \(z\to x\) (same for \(F(t-|\cdot)\)).
Kernel
\(K\ge0\) bounded, symmetric, support \([-1,1]\), \(\int K=1\). Write \(R(K)=\int K^2(u)\,du\).
Bandwidth
\(h=h_n\to0\), \(nh\to\infty\).
What we study
The numerator of the cedf, \(\tilde X_n(t)=\frac1{nh}\sum_i1\{Y_i\le t\}K\big(\tfrac{X_i-x}{h}\big)\), and its process \(\sqrt{nh}\big(\tilde X_n(t)-\mathbb E\tilde X_n(t)\big)\), \(t\in[0,\infty]\).
  1. The class, and why it is this one
    \[f_{n,t}(y,z)=h^{-1/2}\,1\{y\le t\}\,K\big(\tfrac{z-x}{h}\big).\] Then \(\mathbb G^{(n)}f_{n,t}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{nh}}\sum_i\big(1\{Y_i\le t\}K(\cdot)-\mathbb E[\cdot]\big)=\sqrt{nh}\,\big(\tilde X_n(t)-\mathbb E\tilde X_n(t)\big)\).
  2. Envelope
    The indicator is at most 1 (attained at \(t=\infty\)): \(F_n(y,z)=h^{-1/2}K\big(\tfrac{z-x}{h}\big)\).
  3. B: compute ‖F_n‖²
    \[\|F_n\|^2=\frac1h\mathbb E\Big[K^2\big(\tfrac{X-x}{h}\big)\Big]=\frac1h\int K^2\big(\tfrac{z-x}{h}\big)f(z)\,dz\overset{z=x+hu}{=}\int K^2(u)\,f(x+hu)\,du\ \longrightarrow\ f(x)R(K).\]
    \(dz=h\,du\) cancels the \(1/h\). The limit uses continuity of \(f\) at \(x\) and dominated convergence (\(K^2\) bounded with compact support).
  4. Unknown δ and grid
    Let δ \(\gt0\). Choose \(0=t_0\lt\dots\lt t_k=\infty\) with \(\Delta_j:=F(t_j-\mid x)-F(t_{j-1}\mid x)\le\delta\). Greedy choice: each closed cell \((t_{j-1},t_j]\) has mass \(\ge\delta\), total mass 1, so \(k\le1/\delta+1\).
  5. Brackets
    Increasing in \(t\): for \(t\in[t_{j-1},t_j)\), \(l_j=h^{-1/2}1\{y\le t_{j-1}\}K\ \le f_{n,t}\le\ h^{-1/2}1\{y\lt t_j\}K=u_j\).
  6. A: compute the width²
    \(u_j-l_j=h^{-1/2}1\{t_{j-1}\lt y\lt t_j\}K\big(\tfrac{z-x}h\big)\). Square, take expectation, condition on \(X\): \[\|u_j-l_j\|^2=\frac1h\mathbb E\Big[K^2\big(\tfrac{X-x}h\big)P(t_{j-1}\lt Y\lt t_j\mid X)\Big]=\int K^2(u)\big[F(t_j-|x+hu)-F(t_{j-1}|x+hu)\big]f(x+hu)\,du.\] As \(h\to0\) the bracket tends to \(\Delta_j\) uniformly in \(j\) (by the uniform continuity assumption), so \[\|u_j-l_j\|^2\ \longrightarrow\ \Delta_j\,f(x)R(K)\ \le\ \delta\,f(x)R(K).\]
  7. R: ratio
    \[\frac{\|u_j-l_j\|^2}{\|F_n\|^2}\to\frac{\Delta_j\,f(x)R(K)}{f(x)R(K)}=\Delta_j\le\delta.\]
    \(f(x)R(K)\) cancels completely. That is why the book's chain looks like no factor was inserted: the factor is 1.
  8. S: solve and count
    δ = ε² / 2. Count \(k\le2/\varepsilon^2+1\). Entropy integral as before; grid depends only on \(F(\cdot|x)\) and \(\varepsilon\), so (1.8) is skipped.
  9. Hand-in version

    The envelope is \(F_n=h^{-1/2}K((X-x)/h)\) with \(\|F_n\|^2=\int K^2(u)f(x+hu)\,du\to f(x)R(K)\). For \(\delta\gt0\) choose \(0=t_0\lt\dots\lt t_k=\infty\) with \(\Delta_j=F(t_j-|x)-F(t_{j-1}|x)\le\delta\), \(k\le1/\delta+1\). With \(l_j=f_{n,t_{j-1}}\), \(u_j=h^{-1/2}1\{y\lt t_j\}K\), conditioning on \(X\) gives

    \[\frac{\|u_j-l_j\|^2}{\|F_n\|^2}=\frac{\int K^2(u)\,[F(t_j-|x+hu)-F(t_{j-1}|x+hu)]\,f(x+hu)\,du}{\int K^2(u)f(x+hu)\,du}\to\Delta_j\le\delta.\]

    Taking \(\delta=\varepsilon^2/2\), \(\|u_j-l_j\|\le\varepsilon\|F_n\|\) for \(n\) large, so \(N_{[\,]}\le2/\varepsilon^2+1\) and (1.9) holds; the partition is free of \(n\).

Example 4 · Conditional tail process

Theorem 3.23 · mass version
Setting & definitions
Data
\((Y_i,X_i)\) iid, \(Y\gt0\); \(X\) has density \(g\) (twice continuously differentiable), \(g(x)\gt0\) at the fixed \(x\).
Conditional tail
\(\bar F^z(y)=P(Y\gt y\mid X=z)=L^z(y)\,y^{-1/\gamma(z)}\) for \(z\) near \(x\) (3.14), with Condition 3.1 (Lipschitz \(1/\gamma\), Lipschitz \(\log L^z/\log y\), \(h_n\log u_n^x\to0\)).
Threshold
\(k=k_n\to\infty\), \(k/n\to0\), \(u=u_n^x=F^{\leftarrow,x}(1-k/n)\), so \(\bar F^x(u_n^x)=k/n\). Effective sample size \(nh_n\bar F^x(u_n^x)=kh_n\to\infty\).
Kernel
\(K\) bounded, support \([-1,1]\), \(\int K=1\), \(R(K)=\int K^2\). Bandwidth \(h=h_n\to0\).
\(T^x\) and \(T_n^x\)
Limit tail \(T^x(s)=s^{-1/\gamma(x)}\). Define the kernel-weighted tail ratio \[T_n^x(s)=\frac{\mathbb E\big[1\{Y\gt us\}\,K^2\big(\tfrac{x-X}{h}\big)\big]}{h\,\bar F^x(u)}.\]
Lemma 3.2 (quoted)
For \(s,q\gt0\): \(\dfrac{\mathbb E[1\{Y\gt us\}K^q(\frac{x-X}h)]}{h\bar F^x(u)}\to s^{-1/\gamma(x)}g(x)\int K^q\). With \(q=2\): \(T_n^x(s)\to T^x(s)\,g(x)R(K)\).
  1. The class, and why it is this one
    \[f_{n,t}(y,z)=\big(h\bar F^x(u)\big)^{-1/2}1\{y\gt ut\}K\big(\tfrac{x-z}h\big),\quad t\in[a,b],\ 0\lt a\lt b.\] \(\mathbb G^{(n)}f_{n,t}=\dfrac{\sum_i(\cdots)-n\mathbb E(\cdots)}{\sqrt{nh\bar F^x(u)}}=\sqrt{kh}\,\big(\tilde V_n^x(t)-\mathbb E\tilde V_n^x(t)\big)\), the process \(\mathbb V_n^x\) of Theorem 3.23.
  2. Envelope
    Decreasing in \(t\), sup at \(t=a\): \(F_n(y,z)=(h\bar F^x(u))^{-1/2}1\{y\gt ua\}K\big(\tfrac{x-z}h\big)\).
  3. B: compute ‖F_n‖²
    \[\|F_n\|^2=\frac{\mathbb E\big[1\{Y\gt ua\}K^2(\frac{x-X}h)\big]}{h\bar F^x(u)}=T_n^x(a)\ \xrightarrow{\text{Lemma 3.2},\,q=2}\ a^{-1/\gamma(x)}\,g(x)R(K).\]
  4. Unknown δ and grid
    Let δ \(\gt0\); choose \(a=t_0\lt\dots\lt t_m=b\) with \(T^x(t_{j-1})-T^x(t_j)\le\delta\), \(m\le a^{-1/\gamma(x)}/\delta+1\).
  5. Brackets
    \(l_j=f_{n,t_j}\le f_{n,t}\le f_{n,t_{j-1}}=u_j\) for \(t\in[t_{j-1},t_j]\), and \(u_j-l_j=(h\bar F^x(u))^{-1/2}1\{ut_{j-1}\lt y\le ut_j\}K\).
  6. A: compute the width²
    \[\|u_j-l_j\|^2=\frac{\mathbb E\big[1\{ut_{j-1}\lt Y\le ut_j\}K^2\big]}{h\bar F^x(u)}=T_n^x(t_{j-1})-T_n^x(t_j)\ \longrightarrow\ \big[T^x(t_{j-1})-T^x(t_j)\big]g(x)R(K)\le\delta\,g(x)R(K).\]
    \(1\{ut_{j-1}\lt Y\le ut_j\}=1\{Y\gt ut_{j-1}\}-1\{Y\gt ut_j\}\), then use Lemma 3.2 at the two grid points.
  7. R: ratio
    \[\frac{\|u_j-l_j\|^2}{\|F_n\|^2}=\frac{T_n^x(t_{j-1})-T_n^x(t_j)}{T_n^x(a)}\to\frac{[T^x(t_{j-1})-T^x(t_j)]\,g(x)R(K)}{a^{-1/\gamma(x)}g(x)R(K)}\le\delta\,a^{1/\gamma(x)}.\] \(g(x)R(K)\) cancels, \(a^{-1/\gamma(x)}\) remains, exactly like Example 1.
  8. S: solve and count
    δ = ε² a^(−1/γ(x)) / 2; \(m\le2/\varepsilon^2+1\).
    The notes write \(\varepsilon^2a^{-1/\gamma(x)}/(2g(x))\). With the ratio you see \(g(x)\) cancels; both give \(O(\varepsilon^{-2})\) cells, but the ratio version is the one you can justify line by line.

Example 5 · Your turn: a truncated tail class

Not in the notes · exceedances of \(u_na\) that stay below \(u_nt\)
Setting & definitions
Data & tail
\(Z_i\) iid, positive, continuous, \(\bar F\) regularly varying with index \(1/\gamma\gt0\).
Threshold
\(k\to\infty\), \(k/n\to0\), \(u_n=F^{\leftarrow}(1-k/n)\), \(n\bar F(u_n)=k\).
\(T_n\) and \(T\)
\(T_n(s)=\bar F(u_ns)/\bar F(u_n)\to T(s)=s^{-1/\gamma}\).
Class
\(f_{n,t}(z)=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{u_na\lt z\le u_nt\}\), \(t\in[a,b]\), \(0\lt a\lt b\).

Do the routine yourself first, then reveal.

  1. Direction
    Now increasing in \(t\) (the interval \((u_na,u_nt]\) grows). Brackets \(l_j=f_{n,t_{j-1}}\), \(u_j=f_{n,t_j}\).
  2. Envelope and ‖F_n‖²
    Sup at \(t=b\): \(F_n(z)=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{u_na\lt z\le u_nb\}\), and \[\|F_n\|^2=\frac{P(u_na\lt Z\le u_nb)}{\bar F(u_n)}=\frac{\bar F(u_na)-\bar F(u_nb)}{\bar F(u_n)}=T_n(a)-T_n(b)\to a^{-1/\gamma}-b^{-1/\gamma}.\]
  3. A: width²
    \((u_na,u_nt_{j-1}]\subseteq(u_na,u_nt_j]\), so \(u_j-l_j=\bar F(u_n)^{-1/2}1\{u_nt_{j-1}\lt z\le u_nt_j\}\) and \[\|u_j-l_j\|^2=T_n(t_{j-1})-T_n(t_j)\to T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)\le\delta.\]
  4. R
    \(\dfrac{\|u_j-l_j\|^2}{\|F_n\|^2}\to\dfrac{T(t_{j-1})-T(t_j)}{a^{-1/\gamma}-b^{-1/\gamma}}\le\dfrac{\delta}{a^{-1/\gamma}-b^{-1/\gamma}}\).
  5. S
    δ = ε²(a^(−1/γ) − b^(−1/γ)) / 2.
  6. Count
    Total \(T\)-mass is \(T(a)-T(b)\), so \(m\le(T(a)-T(b))/\delta+1=2/\varepsilon^2+1\). For indicator classes the envelope's \(L^2\)-mass equals the total mass, so the count is always \(2/\varepsilon^2+1\).

Example 6 · Your turn: conditional tail, mesh version

Class of Theorem 3.23 with an equidistant grid on \([a,b]\)
Setting & definitions
Model
As in Example 4: \((Y_i,X_i)\) iid, \(\bar F^z(y)=L^z(y)y^{-1/\gamma(z)}\), density \(g\) of \(X\) with \(g(x)\gt0\), Condition 3.1; \(u=u_n^x=F^{\leftarrow,x}(1-k/n)\); kernel \(K\) on \([-1,1]\), \(R(K)=\int K^2\), \(h=h_n\to0\).
\(T^x\), \(T_n^x\)
\(T^x(s)=s^{-1/\gamma(x)}\), \(T_n^x(s)=\mathbb E[1\{Y\gt us\}K^2(\tfrac{x-X}h)]/(h\bar F^x(u))\to T^x(s)g(x)R(K)\) (Lemma 3.2, \(q=2\)).
Derivative
\(\sup_{s\ge a}|{T^x}'(s)|=\tfrac1{\gamma(x)}a^{-1/\gamma(x)-1}\).
Class
\(f_{n,t}(y,z)=(h\bar F^x(u))^{-1/2}1\{y\gt ut\}K(\tfrac{x-z}h)\), \(t\in[a,b]\).
  1. B: envelope²
    Sup at \(t=a\): \(\|F_n\|^2=T_n^x(a)\to a^{-1/\gamma(x)}g(x)R(K)\).
  2. Grid
    \(t_j=a+j\delta\), \(m=\lceil(b-a)/\delta\rceil\), with δ unknown.
  3. A in terms of the mesh
    \(\|u_j-l_j\|^2=T_n^x(t_{j-1})-T_n^x(t_j)\to[T^x(t_{j-1})-T^x(t_j)]g(x)R(K)\le\tfrac1{\gamma(x)}a^{-1/\gamma(x)-1}\delta\,g(x)R(K)\).
  4. R
    \(\dfrac{A}{B}\le\dfrac{\tfrac1{\gamma(x)}a^{-1/\gamma(x)-1}\delta\,g(x)R(K)}{a^{-1/\gamma(x)}g(x)R(K)}=\dfrac{\delta}{\gamma(x)\,a}\).
  5. S & count
    δ = γ(x) a ε² / 2, \(m=\lceil2(b-a)/(\gamma(x)a\varepsilon^2)\rceil\). Same as Example 2 with \(\gamma\to\gamma(x)\).

6Pitfalls

Comparing A with ε²Under Theorem 1.20 you compare \(A\) with \(\varepsilon^2B\), not with \(\varepsilon^2\). Forgetting \(B\) is exactly the "missing factor".
Wrong end for the envelopeDecreasing class → envelope at the left end \(t=a\). Increasing class → envelope at the right end. B changes accordingly (compare Examples 1 and 5).
ε vs ε²A and B are squared norms, so the ratio is compared with \(\varepsilon^2\). If you work with unsquared norms, compare \(\sqrt{A/B}\) with \(\varepsilon\). Pick one and stick to it.
Solving at exactly ε²The limit ratio must be strictly below \(\varepsilon^2\) to absorb the \(o(1)\). Solve for \(\varepsilon^2/2\).
Grid chosen by T_nChoose cells with the limit \(T\) (or \(F(\cdot|x)\)), never with \(T_n\). Then the grid is free of \(n\) and (1.8) is skipped.
Mesh on an infinite setAn equidistant grid on \([0,\infty)\) needs infinitely many cells. Use the mass version there.
